## Collision and search-clutter observations

This is not an exhaustive market search. It is enough to reject any claim that
the name is globally unique:

- [Unstandard Clothing](https://www.unstd.in/) uses the name in India and has
  established social/search presence.
- [Unstandard Studio](https://en.unstandardstudio.com/) uses the name for a
  Canadian animation business.
- Search results also expose existing `unstandard_studio`, `unstd`, and
  `unstd.clothing`-style handles. These do not by themselves establish a legal
  conflict with a dating product; they do establish naming and handle clutter.

## Required clearance procedure

1. Founder defines launch jurisdictions, goods/services description, and the
   Nice classes to search. A dating/social software service will usually need
   more than a raw word search; class selection must be reviewed by a qualified
   practitioner where the risk warrants it.
2. Search exact, phonetic, spacing, abbreviation, and confusingly similar marks
   in the official databases: [KIPRIS](https://www.kipris.or.kr/) for Korea,
   [USPTO trademark search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) with the
   [USPTO search guide](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search), and
   [EUIPO eSearch](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/) if the EU is in scope.
3. Record query strings, classes, jurisdictions, result references, reviewer,
   timestamp, and disposition in a non-secret operator artifact. “No search
   result from a general web engine” is never an accepted disposition.
4. Check one founder-approved handle string across the exact launch channels.
   Record whether each is available, owned, conflicting, or intentionally
   waived. Do not create accounts without separate authorization.
5. Test `Unstandard` verbally and in writing with at least five target users.
   Record first-heard spelling, pronunciation, and associations without storing
   unnecessary identity data. Resolve the `nonstandard` / `substandard` risk.
6. Founder records `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_RISK`, or `REJECT`, then separately
   authorizes purchase. DNS binding remains a later, reversible release action.
